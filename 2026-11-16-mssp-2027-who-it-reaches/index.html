Who do Medicare's 2027 ACO payment changes reach in their first year?

In the CY2027 Physician Fee Schedule final rule (November 9), CMS scores three of its Shared Savings Program changes together. In BASIC Level E, the highest-risk option below ENHANCED, the ACO's share of savings rises from 50% to 60%. For ENHANCED ACOs that spend less than their region, the regional adjustment to the spending target is weighted 35%, down from a 50% maximum. The prior savings adjustment, a credit for past savings, rises from 50% to 75%. Each applies only to five-year agreements beginning January 1, 2027 or later.

CMS's January 2026 ACO list (data.cms.gov) shows 96 of 511 ACOs scheduled for a new agreement in 2027, 25 in 2028, 66 in 2029, 190 in 2030 and 134 in 2031. ACOs can renew early and new entrants start on the new terms; the list counts neither. CMS projects the three changes will lower Medicare spending by $580 million in 2027 (10th to 90th percentile: $730 million to $450 million) and $4.59 billion over ten years ($6.42 billion to $2.88 billion). The rule does not say how CMS's model treats early renewals or track changes.

When Medicare sets a spending target, what should it reward: being more efficient than your region, or becoming more efficient than you were?
