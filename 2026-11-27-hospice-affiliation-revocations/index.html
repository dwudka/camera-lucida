Fig. 01 · Medicare hospice enrollment

Most hospices Medicare revoked for an affiliation show a tie to a revoked agency in public ownership files

Public files show how the current affiliation rule, which CMS has since widened in a final rule, played out for hospices from 2023 to 2026, and that a tie known in advance showed no clear link to the next revocation.

November 27, 2026 · 8 min read · Views are my own and do not represent my employer.

Of the 153 hospices on CMS's revoked list for an affiliation that poses undue risk, 125 (82%) share an officer, director, manager or owner in CMS's public ownership files with an agency revoked the same day or earlier. Revocations also arrive in waves across tied agencies. A tie known in advance showed no clear link to which California hospice was revoked next. A rule CMS finalized on November 9 widens the affiliation authority.

Who entered and who left

California certified 609 new hospices in 2021 and 546 in 2022, by the Medicare entry dates in CMS's Provider of Services file. Of the 2022 entrants, 17% (90% interval 14% to 19%) had their Medicare agreement terminated within three years in California and 16% (13% to 19%) elsewhere. For hospices that entered from 2014 to 2019 the share was 5.7% in California and 5.2% elsewhere. Leaving out voluntary closures and mergers, the 2022 shares are 12.3% and 6.8%, against 1.6% and 0.7% for 2014 to 2019, which rest on 13 and 7 terminations.

The 2022 cohort's first three years fall in the 2024 to 2026 enforcement period, so this chart cannot separate a weaker cohort from a stricter period.

In last week's post I tested whether 12 facts about each hospice's ownership and enrollment could rank the California hospices revoked in 2025 and 2026. They ranked them no better than years in Medicare alone (AUC 0.637, 90% interval 0.609 to 0.665, against 0.646, 0.619 to 0.677). This piece looks at the owners and managers themselves.

What CMS revokes hospices for

CMS's Revoked Medicare Providers and Suppliers file (extract of October 21, 2026) lists 476 hospice revocations still under a re-enrollment bar. The first ground cited is abuse of billing privileges, 42 CFR 424.535(a)(8), in 183, and an affiliation that poses undue risk, 424.535(a)(19), in 131. In all, 156 rows cite the affiliation ground somewhere in the reason.

The affiliation ground comes from a 2019 program integrity rule (84 FR 47794). It defines an affiliation to include a 5% or greater ownership interest, a partnership interest, operational or managerial control, an officer or director role, and a reassignment of benefits. In plain terms, CMS can revoke an enrollment because of what the people who own or run it have also run, when one of those other providers had its own enrollment revoked, denied or terminated, or carries unpaid Medicare debt, a payment suspension or an exclusion, and CMS judges the affiliation an undue risk.

The ties are in public files

CMS publishes quarterly Hospice and Home Health All Owners files. Each row links an agency to an owner, officer, director or manager by a PECOS ID, with a role code that matches the roles in the 2019 definition. I pooled the 15 quarterly files from April 2023 to October 2026 and counted two agencies as tied when one ID appears in both agencies' records in one of those roles.

For 125 of the 153 hospices revoked for an affiliation (82%, 90% interval 76% to 86%), the files show a tie to an agency revoked the same day or earlier; for 111 (73%, 66% to 78%), to one revoked strictly earlier. The same is true for 20% (17% to 24%) of hospices revoked on other grounds, and 12% (12% to 13%) of hospices never revoked have a tie to some revoked agency. Each of the 125 has a tie through a shared officer, director, manager or controlling party. Only 48 also share a 5% owner or partner.

Some IDs sit on hundreds of agencies. Without the 676 IDs that sit on 20 or more agencies, the three shares are 58% (52% to 65%), 15% (12% to 19%) and 10% (9% to 11%). Without the five IDs that supply the most ties, the first share is 65% (58% to 71%).

My read. The ties these files can show run mostly through the people who manage and direct hospices: every hospice with a visible tie has one through an officer, director or manager, and only 48 also share a 5% owner or partner. Hospices revoked for an affiliation show such a tie four times as often as hospices revoked on other grounds, and the gap holds without the hub IDs. A visible tie qualifies under the rule but may not be the one CMS cited, and the files miss affiliations with clinics, suppliers and practitioners, so how many of CMS's cited ties these files would show is unknown.

What would change this. A later revoked-list extract in which hospices revoked for an affiliation show a tie no more often than hospices revoked on other grounds.

Revocations come in waves

Of the 292 hospices revoked from January 2025 to September 2026, 25% are tied to another hospice revoked in the same window. When I reshuffle which hospices were revoked, within the same state and entry year and among hospices that are or are not tied to another hospice, the share is 18% (90% interval 14% to 22%; 500 draws, p = 0.002). Two thirds of the linked revocations (66%, 57% to 75%) cite the affiliation ground, against 28% (23% to 33%) of the rest.

In the October 2024 snapshot, 34 California hospices were tied to an agency already revoked by October 1, 2024. Six of them (18%, 90% interval 9% to 31%) were revoked from January 2025 to September 2026, against 13% (12% to 14%) of other California hospices; adjusted for years in Medicare, the odds ratio is 1.18 (90% interval 0.48 to 2.32), and six events leave wide room either way. Across all states the six are the only tied hospices revoked: 6 of 43 (14%) against 4.5% of untied hospices, odds ratio 2.09 (0.97 to 3.79), not adjusted for state. California hospices tied only to partners revoked from October to December 2024, after the snapshot, were revoked at 36% (14 of 39; 25% to 49%). Those partners' revocations took effect after the snapshot, so a reader in October 2024 could not have used them; counting them as known, the California odds ratio is 2.12 (1.33 to 3.48), so the result depends on that cutoff. Effective dates can also be set retroactively, so even a partner dated before the snapshot may have been decided later.

My read. In this period a tie described how a wave of enforcement moved through related agencies, and a tie visible in advance did little to mark the California hospices revoked next. A tie to a revoked agency is itself a ground for revocation, so wherever the two line up, part of the reason is mechanical. None of this says anything about the conduct of a tied agency or person, and a revocation is an administrative action, distinct from a finding of fraud.

What would change this. A later snapshot in which hospices tied to an agency revoked before the snapshot date are revoked at a clearly higher rate.

What the November rule changes

The CY2027 home health final rule (91 FR 72022, November 9, 2026) amends the enrollment rules in 42 CFR part 424:

  • Whose ties count. The affiliation definition in 424.502 now covers control exercised through a provider's owning or managing employees and adds "any marketing, business, fulfillment, financial, or managerial relationship." The managing-employee definition, which already listed hospice administrators and medical directors, adds other medical directors, clinical directors, department heads, supervising physicians, nursing directors and alternate administrators. The owner files record ownership, partnership, officer, director and managing-employee roles; they do not record the new paragraph (6) relationships. In the preamble, CMS describes revoking a provider under (a)(19) because its medical director also directed a revoked provider, the kind of tie counted above.
  • How far back. Affiliation disclosure in 424.519 loses its 5-year lookback: a provider discloses affiliations it or its owners and managers have or have had.
  • Where the agency sits. New ground (a)(24) allows revocation when CMS determines an enrollment presents a high risk of fraud, waste or abuse because it sits in a limited area with an excessive number of providers. A new denial ground covers a practice location in the same suite or office as a revoked or denied provider.
  • Hospice leadership. A new denial ground covers a hospice whose medical director or administrator also serves multiple other hospices or is too far away to do the job, or whose medical director lacks an active license in the state.
  • When it takes effect. An (a)(19) revocation will take effect on the date CMS determines it. CMS also notes that it "has, to date, never requested" affiliation disclosures; it has revoked on affiliations providers had not yet reported. The rule's DATES section prints January 4, 2026 for these provisions; CMS's responses to comments twice say the enrollment provisions take effect January 1, 2027. As of November 12, 2026, the eCFR still shows the earlier text of 424.519 and (a)(19).

My read. I treat these changes as taking effect January 1, 2027, the date the preamble gives: the January 4, 2026 in the DATES line falls before the rule's publication, and the eCFR still shows the earlier text.

What would change this. A Federal Register correction that sets another date, or the eCFR taking in the new text of 424.519 and (a)(19) before January 1, 2027.

What does not change

  • The public files. CMS has published the All Owners files every quarter since at least April 2023 with these role codes, so the ties are visible to anyone who joins them.
  • The list's limits. The revoked list keeps only revocations still under a re-enrollment bar after first-level appeal, so older revocations and their ties drop out of view.
  • What a revocation is. It remains an administrative action under 424.535, with appeal rights, and it is distinct from a finding of fraud.

What to do with this

  • Join the owner files to your provider roster. If you contract with or refer to hospices, the quarterly All Owners files show which of them share officers, directors or managers with agencies on CMS's revoked list.
  • Treat a tie as context. In this period a tie known in advance showed no clear link to the next California revocation, and 29% (28% to 31%) of never-revoked California hospices have a tie to some revoked agency.
  • Watch the 2027 lists. Once the November rule is in force, count how often hospices are revoked under (a)(19) for ties older than five years, and under the new location ground.

When an enrollment can be revoked for who runs it, how far back should a provider have to check the people it hires?

How the numbers were computed

Every number comes from code in the connectome build (ownnet.affiliations and ownnet.exits), run on the files above as downloaded on November 14, 2026; this post folder carries copies of the two output files. Agencies are tied at the level of their PECOS ID (PAC ID) when one owner ID appears in both in role codes 01, 03, 25, 34, 35 or 38 to 43; mortgage and security interests and "other" are left out. Intervals are Wilson 90% intervals, which treat hospices as independent; tied hospices are not, so the intervals are narrower than they should be. The permutation test reshuffles revocations 500 times within state, entry year and whether a hospice is tied to another hospice. Terminations within three years count every code in the Provider of Services dictionary's table M2; the figures that leave out voluntary closures drop code 01. Odds ratios come from a logistic model with years in Medicare, with a 500-draw bootstrap. The analysis uses the affiliation definition in force before the November 2026 rule. No agency, owner or person is identified in any output.
