Can public ownership data tell which hospices will lose their Medicare billing privileges?

I used CMS's quarterly hospice enrollment and ownership files and its Provider of Services file, all on data.cms.gov. For every hospice active in July 2023, I recorded 12 facts: years in Medicare, number of owners, recent owner changes, chain size, whether it sits in one of the four states CMS put under extra enrollment oversight in 2023 (Arizona, California, Nevada, Texas), and a few more. I trained a model on what happened through September 2024. Then I scored the 6,331 hospices active in October 2024 and still enrolled in January 2025 against CMS's list of revoked providers: 292 of them were revoked between January 2025 and September 2026.

Across all states the model looks strong. It ranks a revoked hospice above one that was not revoked 86% of the time (AUC 0.862, 90% interval 0.852 to 0.873). A two-fact model, years in Medicare plus whether the hospice is in California, scores 0.866 (0.853 to 0.879), and 256 of the 292 revoked hospices are in California. Within California the full model scores 0.637 (0.609 to 0.665) and years in Medicare alone 0.646 (0.619 to 0.677), too close to tell apart. Adding Medicare utilization gave no gain that held up across data years.

My read. The ownership fields add nothing I can measure beyond where a hospice is and how long it has billed Medicare, and they say little about which California hospice is next. A revocation is an administrative action under 42 CFR 424.535, distinct from a finding of fraud, and CMS's list keeps only revocations still under a re-enrollment bar.

When enforcement concentrates in one state, what should a risk model be allowed to learn from where a provider is located?
